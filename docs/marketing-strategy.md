# LinkMeUp — Launch Marketing Strategy

*Prepared for a fast, low-budget go-to-market. Last updated 2026-08-15.*

---

## 1. Positioning

**One line:** LinkMeUp is the fastest way for Kenyans to find trusted work, or trusted help, nearby — paid instantly via M-Pesa.

**Category:** Local services marketplace (informal economy / "jua kali") — not a freelance/remote-work platform. Think "Uber for getting a fundi," not "Upwork for Kenya."

**Why now:** Kenya already runs on M-Pesa and WhatsApp. The gap isn't payments or phones — it's *discovery and trust*: clients don't know which nearby fundi is reliable, and workers don't have a consistent channel to find paying jobs. LinkMeUp's job is to close that gap, not to educate anyone on mobile money.

**Messaging pillars** (use these consistently across every channel):
1. **Nearby** — jobs and workers sorted by distance, not a nationwide noise feed.
2. **Instant pay** — M-Pesa the moment a job is marked done. No cash, no chasing, no "I'll pay you later."
3. **Trust you can see** — ratings, job history, and optional ID verification, visible before you commit.
4. **No friction to join** — phone number + OTP, no password, no CV, no app-store account wall (PWA install works straight from a browser link).

---

## 2. Audiences

This is a two-sided marketplace — you are launching **two** products to **two** very different audiences at the same time. Treat them as separate campaigns that happen to share one app.

| | **Workers ("Watu wa kazi")** | **Clients** |
|---|---|---|
| Who | Fundis, cleaners, boda riders, electricians, movers, tailors, caterers — mostly informal, mobile-first, price-sensitive, often underemployed rather than unemployed | Households, landlords/property managers, small offices, shopkeepers — people who currently find help by asking a neighbor, a caretaker, or a matatu-stage tout |
| Core need | *"Give me a reliable channel to more paying jobs than I currently get by word of mouth."* | *"Let me find someone I can trust to show up and do the job, without gambling on a stranger."* |
| Primary fear | Wasting airtime/time on jobs that don't pay, or platforms that take a cut with no benefit | Getting a no-show, a bad job, or a scam — and having no recourse |
| Where they are | Boda/matatu stages, hardware shops, WhatsApp groups, jua kali associations, TikTok/Facebook | Estate/compound WhatsApp & Facebook groups, caretaker/agent networks, Jiji/OLX-style classifieds, Google search |
| Acquisition cost tolerance | Must be near-zero — this audience will not respond to paid ads well; word of mouth and physical presence work far better | Some paid acquisition is viable (Google/Facebook ads against high-intent searches like "plumber near me") |

**Critical sequencing point:** a marketplace with no workers is useless to clients, and a marketplace with no jobs is useless to workers. Do **not** run broad client-acquisition marketing before there is real worker supply in that specific area. Launch geographically narrow (see §4) and seed supply first.

## 3. Positioning against alternatives

Don't name specific competitors in customer-facing copy unless you've verified current claims about them — but internally, know what you're up against:

- **Word of mouth / the caretaker's cousin** — the real incumbent for most clients today. Beat this with visible trust signals (ratings, verification) a personal referral can't show, and with a faster response time.
- **WhatsApp groups (estate groups, jua kali groups)** — the real incumbent for most workers today. You don't beat WhatsApp by being another WhatsApp group; you beat it by giving structure (search, distance, ratings, guaranteed pay) that a group chat can't. Consider *feeding into* WhatsApp rather than fighting it (see §5, referral flows via WhatsApp share).
- **Other gig/services apps** — if and when they come up in conversation, differentiate on the M-Pesa-native "pay only when it's done" flow and the fact that this is a PWA (installs instantly from a link, no Play Store friction) *plus* an Android app for users who prefer the store.

## 4. Launch strategy: go hyperlocal, not national

Kenya is too big to launch everywhere at once with no marketing budget. Pick **one dense, mixed-income area** (a single estate, a couple of adjacent estates, or one town) where:
- You (or someone on the team) can physically show up.
- There's a visible concentration of both informal workers and the clients who'd hire them (e.g., a residential estate next to a jua kali cluster or hardware market).

### Phase 0 — Supply seeding (Weeks 1–2, before any client marketing)
Goal: **50–100 real, complete worker profiles** in the launch area before you spend a single shilling telling clients to come look.

- Walk the target area's boda stage, hardware shops, and jua kali sheds. Sign workers up in person, on the spot, on your phone — don't hand out flyers and hope.
- Partner with 2–3 jua kali association leaders or boda stage chairs; get their buy-in and let them vouch for the app to their members. Trust transfers from a respected local figure faster than any ad.
- Offer a simple, honest incentive for the first cohort: e.g., "First 100 verified workers get a badge and priority placement for 30 days" (real, deliverable — don't promise cash you can't pay out from an unlaunched product).
- Every signed-up worker should leave with the app installed and their profile *complete* (photo, skill, area) — an empty profile doesn't convert. Do this in person; don't rely on them finishing it later.

### Phase 1 — Soft client launch, same area (Weeks 2–4)
Goal: first 20–30 completed, paid jobs.

- Post in the estate/compound WhatsApp and Facebook groups you're already a member of, or get a resident to post on your behalf ("I found this, works well, thought you'd want to know" reads far better than an obvious plug).
- Print a small number of A5 flyers/posters for estate noticeboards, gate/security desks, and shared laundry areas — QR code straight to `/signup?role=CLIENT`, one clear headline ("Need a fundi today? Nearby, verified, pay by M-Pesa.").
- Personally handle the first ~20 jobs' worth of support — reply fast, fix friction immediately, ask every client and worker for a review afterward. Word of mouth from a smooth first job is your best channel; a bad first experience for the first cohort can kill local trust before you've started.

### Phase 2 — Expand radius (Month 2+)
Only once the launch area has a healthy ratio of active workers to jobs (rough target: enough worker supply that a posted job gets a response within minutes, most hours of the day):
- Repeat Phase 0–1 in an adjacent area.
- Start light paid acquisition (Google Search ads on "[service] near me [area]" terms, Facebook/Instagram ads geo-targeted to the new area) now that there's supply to actually serve the demand it generates.
- Turn on referrals (§5) as the primary growth engine so you're not paying for every new user.

## 5. Channels & tactics

### WhatsApp (highest-leverage channel in this market)
- Add a "Share LinkMeUp" action from the app (a `wa.me` link with pre-filled text) so a happy client/worker can forward it to their own group in one tap. This is likely your single best growth channel — prioritize it if it doesn't already exist.
- A WhatsApp Business number for support builds trust fast in this market; a bare email address does not.

### Facebook & Instagram
- Facebook groups (estate groups, "buy and sell" groups, jua kali groups) are where this audience already is. Organic posts (genuine, not obviously an ad) outperform paid boosts early on.
- Instagram/Facebook Reels: short "book a job → get paid" demo clips, and short worker-spotlight clips ("Meet James, an electrician in [area], now getting 3x the jobs he used to").

### TikTok
- Same short-form content as above, aimed at the worker side especially — "how I get paid same-day now" style content performs well and costs nothing but time.

### Local / vernacular radio
- Cheap, high-reach, and specifically strong with the informal-worker audience who may not be the heaviest social-media users. A short spot on a local/vernacular station covering the launch area, timed to Phase 1, is a good low-cost buy.

### Physical / offline
- Boda boda and matatu-stage presence: branded stickers/small banners at stages you've partnered with (cheap, hyper-local, always-on visibility).
- Hardware shops, paint shops, and building-material suppliers: ask to leave a small stack of QR flyers at the till — their customers are disproportionately your worker audience.

### Search & the new marketing website
- The `/` marketing page now built (hero, how-it-works, features, trust, CTA) is your SEO landing surface. Prioritize:
  - A Google Business Profile for LinkMeUp once there's a real office/contact point, listed under "local services."
  - Basic on-page SEO: make sure the site's `<title>`/description target phrases like "find a fundi near me," "hire a plumber Nairobi," "get paid M-Pesa jobs" once you pick the launch city/area — update `src/app/layout.tsx` metadata and add area-specific copy if you expand to multiple towns.
  - Submit the site to Google Search Console early so indexing isn't a bottleneck later.

### Referral program (build once, compounds forever)
- Simple mechanic: both sides get a small, real benefit for a successful referral (e.g., a completed-job discount credit, or a priority-placement period for workers) — avoid cash referral bounties until there's a finance/fraud process to support them.
- Surface it in the app (Account page, post-job-completion moment) — that's the highest-intent point to ask someone to share.

### App store presence (Android)
- The Capacitor Android wrapper already exists in this repo (`capacitor.config.ts`, `android/`). Getting a real Play Store listing live (even a simple one) adds legitimacy that matters to less tech-savvy clients who trust "an app" more than "a website" — plan this alongside Phase 1, not as an afterthought.
- Store listing copy should mirror the messaging pillars in §1; use the new icon and screenshots of the actual product (not mockups).

## 6. Trust & safety messaging (do this in parallel, not after)

For an informal-economy marketplace, trust *is* the product. Bake these into every piece of marketing, not just the app:

- Always show the "pay only when the job's marked done" mechanic explicitly — it's your strongest trust argument for clients and should be in headline copy, not buried in an FAQ.
- Actively push worker verification uptake (it exists via the admin verification queue) — a visible "Verified" badge is a conversion lever for clients; consider making it a launch-area requirement for the first cohort so early impressions are strong.
- Publish a simple, honest safety page (what verification actually checks, what happens if a job goes wrong, how disputes are handled) before wide client marketing — don't imply guarantees the current dispute-handling process can't back up.

## 7. Metrics to track from day one

| Metric | Why it matters | Early target (first 30–60 days, single launch area) |
|---|---|---|
| Worker profiles completed (not just signed up) | Supply is the constraint early on | 50–100 |
| % of workers verified | Trust lever for clients | Rising trend, not a fixed number early |
| Jobs posted vs. jobs filled | Marketplace liquidity | Filled rate should climb toward most jobs getting a response within minutes |
| Time-to-first-response on a posted job | Client experience | Track and try to drive down |
| Completed jobs paid via M-Pesa | The core loop actually working | Track weekly growth, not absolute number |
| Repeat clients / repeat-hire of same worker (Network usage) | Retention signal | Any repeat usage in month one is a good sign this early |
| Referral share actions | Growth engine health | Track once the WhatsApp share flow ships |

## 8. Budget allocation (assuming a small/no-budget launch)

| Category | % of budget | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Physical/local (flyers, stage/shop partnerships, small radio spot) | 40% | Highest ROI at this stage; mostly cash, not agencies |
| Paid social/search (Phase 2 onward only) | 25% | Hold until worker supply can meet demand it generates |
| Incentives (worker onboarding perks, referral credits) | 20% | Real, deliverable rewards only — protect trust over growth |
| Tools (WhatsApp Business, SMS/OTP costs at scale, analytics) | 10% | OTP delivery cost scales with signups — budget for it before a marketing push, not after |
| Contingency | 5% | |

- [ ] Stand up a WhatsApp Business number for support.
